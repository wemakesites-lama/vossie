# CampusCart final presentation: the plan

5 November 2026 · a plan to agree before any slide is made · 10 minutes, four speakers, one live demo

## 1. What this presentation has to do

The final is judged on the Stage 2 rubric, "Prototype and Execution". Every criterion wants to *see* it, not hear about it.

| Criterion | Weight | Where this plan earns it |
|---|---|---|
| Problem and target market, with evidence | 15% | The 60-second "why", and the evidence beat |
| Innovation that differentiates | 15% | Three AI moments live in the demo |
| Intuitive, working user journeys | 15% | The three-act demo: buy, sell, run |
| Stable prototype, scalability | 15% | The demo itself, then 30 seconds of "built to last" |
| Operating model, user acquisition, sustainability and growth | 15% | The business section: costs, subscription, extra revenue |
| Impact on Hub students, measurable | 10% | The impact beat before the close |
| User testing, with improvements made | 5% | "What students told us, and what we changed" |
| Professional presentation, live demo, whole team | 10% | Four speakers, rehearsed handovers, a backup for the demo |

The earlier pitch explained the idea. This one shows a finished product and proves it can pay for itself.

## 2. The Apple playbook, applied

What Apple's keynotes do, and what we copy:

- **One sentence first.** Jobs gave every product a headline you could repeat ("1,000 songs in your pocket"). Ours: **"Your campus, open for business."** Said in the first 30 seconds and the last 10.
- **The rule of three.** Three parts, three features, three numbers. Never four. The whole talk is three acts: *Why. What. How it lasts.* The demo is three acts: *Buy. Sell. Run.*
- **One idea per slide.** A slide is a billboard: one line, or one number, or one screen. No bullet lists in the main flow. The detail lives in what the speaker says.
- **Show, don't tell.** The demo is the centre of the talk, about a third of the time. Features appear on a real phone screen, not in a list.
- **Numbers with meaning.** Never "R1,226 a month". Say **"less than one textbook a month"** and **"about 60 cents a student"**.
- **A moment they'll repeat.** One planned "wow": a photo of something in the room becomes a finished listing in seconds.
- **A villain.** Not WhatsApp itself (the lecturer was right). The villain is *the scroll*: a good listing gone in minutes, a buyer who can't tell who to trust.
- **Rehearsed until it looks easy.** Apple rehearses for weeks. We do five full timed runs, two with the real projector.
- **Premium look.** Dark canvas, huge type, the product on real device frames, generous empty space, one accent colour. The new CampusCart mark, not the crest.

## 5. The demo, in three acts

One phone-sized window for acts 1 and 2, one laptop-width window for act 3. Signed in before the talk starts. About 3:20 in total.

**Act 1: Buy (1:10).** The line: *"Search the way you talk."*
1. Type "cheap calculator under R300 at Midrand". It becomes calculator, Midrand, cheapest first.
2. Open the Casio. Price, pickup point, "See it before you pay", the seller's badge.
3. Message the seller: "I can pay a deposit now". The scam check stops it. Pause. Let them read it.

**Act 2: Sell (1:10).** The line: *"A listing in one photo."* This is the wow.
1. New listing. Take a photo of a real object on the table (bring a calculator; do not improvise).
2. "Fill in from my photo". Title, category, condition and price fill themselves, tagged "Suggested".
3. Try to publish a "replica" title. The listing check stops it.
4. The seller's home: what needs you, the last 7 days, and "This week" from Shop coach.

**Act 3: Run (1:00).** The line: *"The Hub sees everything it needs, and nothing it shouldn't."*
1. The console's Today: one shop waiting, with its age.
2. Approve it. The badge clears.
3. Last week's numbers by campus, with small counts hidden.

**Insurance.**
- A screen recording of all three acts, cut to the same length, one click away.
- The demo campus loaded with believable shops and listings (the demo activity script for vossie-pi exists as of today).
- Notifications off, one browser profile, every tab already open, the phone window sized to the projector.
- The demo accounts tested the evening before and an hour before.
- If the AI call is slow, keep talking: "it's reading the photo on the server, so the phone stays fast". If it fails, the recording.

## 6. What it costs to run

Estimates, at R17 to the dollar. They exclude people's time, and they should be checked against the providers' pricing pages before they go on a slide.

| Size | Active students | Shops | Hosting | Database | Email | AI | Monthly total | Per student |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot, one campus | 500 | 30 | R340 | R425 | R0 | R26 | **about R800** | R1.61 |
| Year one, 10% of Eduvos | 2,000 | 120 | R340 | R425 | R340 | R106 | **about R1,230** | 61c |
| Growth, about 40% | 8,000 | 480 | R680 | R680 | R340 | R424 | **about R2,140** | 27c |
| Every student | 20,000 | 1,200 | R1,360 | R1,275 | R340 | R1,060 | **about R4,050** | 20c |

What's behind the numbers:

- **Hosting:** Vercel Pro, $20 a seat, with 1 TB of transfer included. The app sends about 150 KB of code on a first visit, so traffic stays inside the allowance for a long time.
- **Database:** Supabase Pro, $25 a month, with 100,000 monthly users, 8 GB of database and 100 GB of files included. Eduvos has about 21,000 students, so the user allowance is never the limit; a bigger machine is.
- **Email:** Resend. Free covers 3,000 emails a month but only 100 a day, which a busy first week breaks, so Pro ($20) from year one.
- **AI:** the project's own estimate is under R100 a month for all the AI features at 2,000 active students, because each call is small, cached where it can be, and capped per account. Shop coach adds about R5 per 100 shops.
- **Shops:** assumed at 6% of active students.

**The story for the slide:** the cost per student *falls* as it grows, from R1.61 to 20 cents, because the base fees are fixed and the usage is tiny. Twenty-five times the students costs five times as much.

## 7. How it pays for itself

### The recommendation: buyers never pay. Shops choose a plan.

A marketplace only works when buyers turn up. A fee at the door sends them back to WhatsApp, which is free. So the subscription sits with the people who earn from the platform.

| | **Starter** | **Plus** |
|---|---|---|
| Price | Free, always | **R29 a month** |
| Shop, Hub approval, badges, messaging | Yes | Yes |
| Live listings | Up to 5 | Unlimited |
| "Fill in from my photo" and photo tips | 3 a month | Unlimited (fair use) |
| Shop coach: three actions a week | No | Yes |
| Shop insights: enquiries, reply times, followers over time | Last 7 days | Full history |

**The line for the slide:** *"One sale pays for the year."* A R29 plan is R348 a year; one R350 textbook sale covers it.

**Trust is never for sale.** Hub approval, Verified, the three badges and the fair Featured draw are identical on both plans. That sentence should be said out loud: it protects the product's whole promise.

### Break-even

Card fees through Paystack are 2.9% plus R1 per payment, so a R29 plan nets about R26.90.

| Size | Monthly cost | Paying shops needed at R29 | That is |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot | about R800 | 31 | more shops than the pilot has |
| Year one | about R1,230 | **46** | 38% of 120 shops |
| Growth | about R2,140 | 80 | 17% of 480 shops |
| Every student | about R4,050 | 151 | 13% of 1,200 shops |

**The honest reading, and it is a strength to say it:**

- The **pilot does not pay for itself** on subscriptions. It costs about R800 a month, and the Hub or a single campus sponsor carries it. That is the ask.
- From **year one, 46 paying shops** cover everything.
- At scale, a quarter of shops on Plus brings in about R8,000 a month against R4,050 of cost. The surplus funds a part-time student maintainer, which is itself a Hub outcome.

### If the team meant "every student subscribes"

It is worth knowing the numbers: at R5 a month, 2,000 students would bring in about R9,700. But it puts a paywall in front of buyers, and paid conversion on free consumer apps is usually a few percent, so the real figure would be a fraction of that while the listings went unseen. I would not present it. If the team wants a buyer-side product later, make it optional: early alerts and an ad-free experience for R10.

## 8. More ways to earn, ranked

| Idea | How it works | Rough value | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campus partners** | A print shop, laptop repairer or food outlet near campus sponsors a Collection, the Starter pack or a Launchpad event, labelled | R1,000 to R2,500 a campus a month | Best: covers the pilot alone |
| **Sponsored shop** | One clearly labelled "Sponsored" place per campus, separate from the fair Featured row, Verified shops only | R150 a week per campus | Good, with the label |
| **Boosts** | Lift one listing for 3 days | R10 each | Good: tiny, student-priced |
| **The Hub's licence** | Eduvos pays a flat fee per campus for the console, the numbers and the mentoring tools | R1,500 to R2,000 a campus a month | The anchor, if Eduvos adopts it |
| **Event partners** | A bank's or network's student brand sponsors pitch night | R5,000 an event | Occasional |
| Payments and escrow, with a fee | Pay in the app, released on collection | 3% to 5% | Later: needs a licence partner and changes the safety model |

**What we will not do:** sell student data, sell Verified or badges, or show ads in messages. Say it on the slide. Judges from the Hub will care.

## 10. Decisions for Lama and the team

1. **Who pays the subscription?** Recommended: shops only, with a free Starter plan. Buyers free.
2. **The price.** Recommended R29 a month. R19 needs 72 paying shops in year one; R49 needs 27 but is a harder sell to a student.
3. **Who carries the pilot's R800 a month?** The Hub, or one campus sponsor. This becomes the ask.
4. **The wow.** Recommended: the photo that becomes a listing. Alternative: a judge's own sentence typed into search.
5. **Where it's presented from.** Your own Mac in Keynote allows the brand fonts and smooth transitions. A venue PC in PowerPoint means standard fonts. This changes how I build the file.
6. **The speaker split** in section 3.
7. **Build the Plans page before the day**, or present the model as a plan only.

## 11. The look

- **Canvas:** deep navy to near-black, the app's own Night theme colours. White type. Sky blue as the single accent.
- **Type:** one display line per slide, 60 to 90 point. Supporting line at 24. Nothing under 20 in the main flow.
- **Product:** real screens in a clean phone frame, large, one per slide, sometimes three in a row.
- **Numbers:** one per slide, enormous, with a seven-word caption.
- **The feature wall:** one dense, beautiful grid of sixteen small tiles, shown once. It is the only busy slide, and it is busy on purpose.
- **Motion:** simple fades and one slide-to-slide morph on the phone. Nothing that can fail on another machine.
- **No:** bullet lists, tables or footers in the main flow. Tables live in the backup slides.
